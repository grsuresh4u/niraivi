The craft

What makes a true Kanchipuram

A Kanchipuram silk saree is defined by where it is woven, what it is woven from and how it is woven. Here is what to look for.

i.

Pure mulberry silk

Woven from pure mulberry silk, with the yarn dyed before weaving for deep, lasting colour and the weight a Kanchipuram is known for.

ii.

The korvai join

Body and border are often woven in contrasting colours and interlocked by hand on the loom — the korvai technique. Turn the saree over and the join is still visible.

iii.

Zari, honestly told

Zari is metallic thread wound around a silk core. Pure zari uses silver coated with gold; tested zari is a more affordable alternative. Ask us and we will tell you which your saree has.

iv.

Protected by name

Kanchipuram silk holds a Geographical Indication (GI) tag. The name belongs to sarees handwoven in the Kanchipuram region using its traditional methods.

The language of the loom

Motifs woven with meaning

Kanchipuram designs draw on the temples and traditions of the town. These are the motifs you will see again and again.

Temple border

கோபுரம்

Stepped like a gopuram, the temple border is the signature of a Kanchipuram and a symbol of protection and auspiciousness.

Peacock

மயில்

The vahana of Lord Murugan, the peacock stands for grace and beauty and is a favourite on bridal pallus.

Parrot

கிளி

In Tamil tradition the parrot is a symbol of love, which is why it appears so often in wedding sarees.

Rudraksham

ருத்ராட்சம்

A border of sacred rudraksha beads, associated with Lord Shiva and with devotion.

Kattam

கட்டம்

Checks woven in silk and zari — a classic Kanchipuram pattern, elegant for festive and everyday wear.

Buyer's guide

How to know your Kanchipuram is real

Imitations are common, especially online. These are the checks experienced buyers use.

  • Look for the Silk Mark labelThe Silk Mark is issued under the Central Silk Board and certifies pure natural silk.
  • Turn it overOn a korvai saree, the join between body and border is visible on the reverse. Printed or stuck-on borders are a warning sign.
  • Check the zariReal zari has a soft, deep glow rather than a bright, glittery shine. Ask the seller to state the zari type.
  • Feel the weightHandwoven Kanchipuram silk has body and weight. A saree that feels flimsy, especially at the border, deserves a closer look.
  • Question the priceHandloom silk and zari are costly to make. A price that seems too good to be true usually is.

Saree care

Caring for your silk

Looked after well, a Kanchipuram lasts for generations.

  • Dry clean onlyAvoid washing at home, especially for the first few years.
  • Wrap it in cottonStore it in a soft cotton or muslin cloth, never in plastic, so the silk can breathe.
  • Refold every few monthsChange the folds regularly so the zari does not crack along the creases.
  • Keep perfume awaySpray perfume before you drape, and keep it off the zari to stop it tarnishing.
  • Air it in the shadeAfter wearing, air the saree away from direct sunlight before you put it away.
